The Architect-Level Summary


Question 1
 
Why not per-tab sessions?
 
Because browsers were designed around
origin-level identity, not tab-level identity.
 
Per-tab sessions would severely damage usability
and still would not solve all request-forgery problems.
 
Therefore browsers kept shared cookies and the industry
introduced CSRF protections.
 
────────────────────────────────────
 
Question 2
 
Doesn't CORS nullify SOP?
 
No.
 
SOP does not prevent sending requests.
 
SOP prevents reading responses.
 
CORS is a controlled exception mechanism that allows a
server to explicitly tell the browser which origins are
allowed to read cross-origin responses.
 
Without SOP:
 
Everything readable.
 
With SOP:
 
Nothing readable cross-origin.
 
With SOP + CORS:
 
Only explicitly approved origins are readable.
The next question that naturally follows from this is:



If SOP already prevents evil.com from reading bank.com,
how exactly does XSS bypass SOP and gain access to
cookies, DOM, CSRF tokens and local storage?
That's where the browser security model becomes truly clear.
